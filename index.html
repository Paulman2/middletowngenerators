Does the weekly self‑test actually test anything?

It proves the engine can idle. That is all it proves.

Twelve minutes

Your generator runs for about twelve minutes once a week, with no load on it, and shuts off.

  • The transfer switchThe self‑test never asks it to pick up your house.not tested
  • The battery under loadA battery can read full voltage and still fail the moment it is asked to crank.not tested
  • The oilFour years old runs as quietly as new oil, right up until it does not.not tested
  • The wiringMice get into an enclosure over the winter. An idle test will not find them.not tested

Most no‑starts get discovered during the outage. That is the worst possible time to find out, and the hardest time to get parts or a technician.

What happens on a service visit?

Work follows the manufacturer’s schedule for your model. You get a written record of everything performed, and a written estimate before any repair. Never a verbal number.

  • A simulated power failureYour house gets put on generator power while I am standing there. It is the test that matters and the one almost nobody runs.every visit
  • Battery load testNot a voltage reading. Voltage lies on a battery about to fail.every visit
  • Oil, filters and plugsOil and oil filter, air filter and spark plugs at the interval for your model. Valve clearance when it comes due.every visit
  • Fuel and wiringFuel pressure, fuel lines and connections, grounding and conduit condition.every visit
  • Enclosure inspectionRodent damage, rust, corrosion and water intrusion. Winter is hard on an outdoor enclosure and the damage is rarely visible from the driveway.every visit
  • Controller and run hoursModel, serial and install year recorded. Run hours and the fault log read off the controller, and the exercise schedule confirmed.every visit

Where do you work, and what will you turn down?

What else do people ask?

What is the difference between the $29 and $39 plans?

Both get the same thorough annual service. Priority adds a second visit, a spring pre‑storm check before storm season, plus priority scheduling during outages, no trip charge on service calls and 10% off parts. Most people take Priority because the spring check is what catches a dead battery before July rather than during an outage.

How fast will you get here when the power goes out?

I will not give you a number, and you should be skeptical of anyone who does. After a storm that takes out the county, nobody can reach every customer quickly. That is physics, not effort. What Priority members get is priority scheduling: members go ahead of non‑members in the queue. The honest answer is that the real protection is the service visit beforehand, not the response afterward.
