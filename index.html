What the self-test misses

A twelve-minute idle is not a test of anything that fails in a real outage.

  1. It does not prove the transfer switch will actually pick up your house.
  2. It does not prove the battery can crank under load. A battery can read full voltage and still fail the moment it's asked to work.
  3. It does not prove the oil isn't four years old.
  4. It does not prove mice haven't been in the wiring over the winter.

Most no-starts get discovered during the outage — the worst possible time to find out, and the hardest time to get parts or a technician.

What actually gets done

A simulated power failure

Your house gets put on generator power while I'm standing there. This is the test that matters, and it's the one almost nobody runs. A generator that passes its weekly self-test has only proven it can idle.

Battery load test

Not a voltage reading. Voltage lies on a battery about to fail — it will show full charge right up until the moment it's asked to crank an engine.

Oil, filters and plugs

Oil and oil filter, air filter, and spark plugs at the manufacturer's interval for your model. Valve clearance checked when it comes due.

Fuel and wiring

Fuel pressure reading, fuel lines and connections, plus grounding and conduit condition.

Enclosure inspection

Rodent damage, rust, corrosion and water intrusion. Winter is hard on an outdoor enclosure and the damage is rarely visible from the driveway.

Controller and run hours

Model, serial and install year recorded. Run hours and the fault log read off the controller, and the exercise schedule confirmed to still be running.

Questions

Straight answers

My generator runs its test every week and sounds fine. Why do I need this?

Because the weekly self-test only proves the engine can idle for twelve minutes with no load on it. It never engages the transfer switch, never asks the battery to crank under real demand, and never puts your house on generator power. Those are the three things that actually fail, and the self-test checks none of them.

What's the difference between the $29 and $39 plans?

Both get the same thorough annual service. Priority adds a second visit — a spring pre-storm check before storm season — plus priority scheduling during outages, no trip charge on service calls, and 10% off parts. Most people take Priority because the spring check is what catches a dead battery before July rather than during an outage.

How fast will you get here when the power goes out?

I won't give you a number, and you should be skeptical of anyone who does. After a storm that takes out the county, nobody can reach every customer quickly — that's physics, not effort. What Priority members get is priority scheduling: members go ahead of non-members in the queue. The honest answer is that the real protection is the service visit beforehand, not the response afterward.

Will you service my brand of generator?

I service residential air-cooled standby generators from the major manufacturers. Tell me the make and model when you call and I'll tell you honestly whether it's something I work on.

What happens if you find something broken?

When does my first visit happen?

Within 60 to 90 days of signing up. Service is scheduled, not emergency-driven, which is what keeps the route tight and the price where it is.
